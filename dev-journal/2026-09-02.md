### What did I, Claude Code, or my agents learn from this

- **Read the prior day's journal before writing findings, not after.** Three of the day's
  candidate findings were already recorded as decisions or watch-outs. Without that
  cross-reference the review would have re-litigated a tradeoff the author had already
  weighed and logged — which reads as the reviewer not having done the reading.
- **A subagent's confident causal claim is the thing to check first.** The "collateral, not
  intended" framing on the OG change was stated with specifics (the in-code comment never
  mentions OG, the commit message doesn't either) and was still wrong about intent. Both facts
  were true; the conclusion drawn from them wasn't. Evidence quality and inference quality are
  separate checks.
- **A wrong test name costs more than a missing test.** The strongest finding of the day
  wasn't an uncovered branch — it was a test whose name claims to cover the exact thing that
  changed, sitting where the assertion used to be. A gap gets found; a false positive gets
  trusted.
